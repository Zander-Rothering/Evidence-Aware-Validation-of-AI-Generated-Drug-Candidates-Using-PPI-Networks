# EDA — MolGPT Generated HMGCR Inhibitor Candidates

> Part of: Evidence-Aware Validation of AI-Generated Drug Candidates Using PPI Networks  
> Target: HMGCR — HMG-CoA Reductase (ChEMBL CHEMBL1781)  
> Generated by: MolGPT fine-tuned on 665 ChEMBL HMGCR inhibitors

---

## What This EDA Does

This notebook evaluates AI-generated statin-like compounds from MolGPT against two baselines:

1. ZINC250K — a general purpose drug-like compound library (100,000 compounds)
2. ChEMBL HMGCR reference set — 735 experimentally validated HMGCR inhibitors

The EDA answers three questions:

- Are the MolGPT generated compounds structurally similar to known HMGCR inhibitors?
- Do they pass standard drug-likeness filters (Lipinski, QED, PAINS)?
- How do they compare to ZINC250K as a candidate source?

## EDA Steps

**Step 1 — Load ChEMBL reference**  
Fetch 735 HMGCR inhibitor SMILES from ChEMBL API (target CHEMBL1781, IC50 assays only). These are the gold standard compounds the pipeline compares against.

**Step 2 — Encode fingerprints**  
Convert all SMILES to 2048-bit Morgan fingerprints (ECFP4, radius=2) using RDKit. Fingerprints are the numerical representation used for similarity scoring.

**Step 3 — Load and score ZINC250K**  
Load 100,000 compounds from ZINC250K. Score each against all 735 ChEMBL reference fingerprints using `BulkTanimotoSimilarity` (C++ backed, fast). Keep maximum score per compound.

**Step 4 — Load and score MolGPT candidates**  
Load generated compounds from `generated_statins_10k.csv`. Filter trivial compounds with fewer than 10 atoms. Score against ChEMBL reference set.

**Step 5 — Filter hits**  
Apply Tanimoto threshold of 0.40 to identify statin-like candidates from both libraries.

**Step 6 — Compute descriptors on hits**  
For each hit compute MW, LogP, HBD, HBA, QED, Lipinski violations and PAINS flag. Descriptors computed on hits only — not on all 100,000 candidates.

**Step 7 — Summary and visualisations**  
Compare ZINC250K and MolGPT across Tanimoto distribution, QED, filter funnels, MW vs Tanimoto scatter, and 2D structure diagrams.

---

## Results

MolGPT was fine-tuned on 665 HMGCR inhibitor SMILES over 50 epochs, with training loss converging from 1.44 to 0.80. An initial generation of 1,000 candidates produced 13 non-trivial valid compounds. To increase the candidate pool, a second generation run of 10,000 candidates was performed, producing 125 non-trivial valid compounds. Of these, 101 scored above Tanimoto 0.40 and 70 scored above 0.60 against the ChEMBL HMGCR reference set, with 99 passing both Lipinski and PAINS filters. The mean Tanimoto of 0.659 is nearly 3x higher than ZINC250K (0.225), which produced only 9 hits above 0.60 from 100,000 candidates.

- Training compounds: 665 (after cleaning)
- Epochs: 50
- Training loss: 1.44 to 0.80
- Generation run 1: 1,000 candidates → 13 non-trivial valid compounds
- Generation run 2: 10,000 candidates → 125 non-trivial valid compounds
- Hits above Tanimoto 0.40: 101 / 125 (81%)
- Hits above Tanimoto 0.60: 70 / 125 (56%)
- Lipinski pass: 101 / 101 (100%)
- PAINS clean: 99 / 101 (98%)
- Clean candidates: 99
- Mean Tanimoto vs ChEMBL: 0.659 vs 0.225 for ZINC250K
- ZINC hits above 0.60: 9 / 100,000 (0.009%)

## Key Finding

Target focused generation using MolGPT fine-tuned on 665 HMGCR inhibitors produces structurally relevant candidates at a hit rate of 56% above Tanimoto 0.60, compared to 0.009% for the general purpose ZINC250K library. All generated hits pass Lipinski and 98% pass PAINS filters, confirming that domain specific generative models substantially outperform random screening libraries for drug candidate generation.

## References

Each reference below explains why it is included — what specific method, dataset, or tool in the EDA came from that paper.

**[1] Bagal S et al. MolGPT. J Chem Inf Model. 2022. https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01507**  
MolGPT is the generative model that produced the 125 AI-generated HMGCR candidate compounds evaluated in this EDA. We cite this paper because all generated SMILES in `generated_statins_10k.csv` were produced by this model.

**[2] Gaulton A et al. ChEMBL. Nucleic Acids Res. 2012. https://doi.org/10.1093/nar/gkr777**  
ChEMBL is the source of the 735 HMGCR inhibitor SMILES used as the gold standard reference set for all Tanimoto scoring. It is also the source of the 665 training compounds used to fine-tune MolGPT. We cite this paper to give credit to the database and let the reader verify where our reference set came from.

**[3] Irwin JJ et al. ZINC. J Chem Inf Model. 2012. https://doi.org/10.1021/ci3001277**  
ZINC250K provided the 100,000 general purpose drug-like compounds used as the baseline comparison library. We cite this paper to justify why we chose ZINC as the comparison and to let the reader verify what ZINC250K contains.

**[4] Rogers D, Hahn M. ECFP Fingerprints. J Chem Inf Model. 2010. https://doi.org/10.1021/ci100050t**  
Every Tanimoto score in the EDA is computed from Morgan/ECFP4 fingerprints at radius=2 with 2048 bits. The specific choices of radius and bit size come from this paper. We cite it to justify why we used these parameters rather than different ones.

**[5] Bickerton GR et al. QED. Nat Chem. 2012. https://doi.org/10.1038/nchem.1243**  
QED (Quantitative Estimate of Drug-likeness) is the drug-likeness score used in Plot 3, Plot 8, and the filter funnel. The 0.34 minimum threshold applied in the pipeline comes directly from this paper. We cite it so the reader can verify what QED measures and why 0.34 was chosen.

**[6] Baell JB, Holloway GA. PAINS. J Med Chem. 2010. https://doi.org/10.1021/jm901137j**  
The PAINS filter applied in Step 6 and shown in the filter funnel plots is based on 480 problematic substructure patterns defined in this paper. We cite it to justify why a compound flagged by PAINS is considered a liability and should be excluded.

**[7] Landrum G. RDKit: Open-source cheminformatics. rdkit.org. 2006.**  
RDKit is the cheminformatics library used for every operation in the EDA — SMILES parsing, fingerprint generation, Tanimoto scoring, descriptor computation, QED, PAINS filtering and 2D structure drawing. We cite it because without RDKit none of the analysis would be possible.

**[8] BulkTanimotoSimilarity C++ API. RDKit docs. https://www.rdkit.org/docs/source/rdkit.DataStructs.cDataStructs.html**  
`BulkTanimotoSimilarity` is the specific RDKit function used in Steps 3 and 4 to score compounds against all 735 reference fingerprints in a single C++ call. We cite it separately from RDKit because it is the key performance decision — without it scoring 100,000 ZINC compounds would be 10 to 20 times slower.
